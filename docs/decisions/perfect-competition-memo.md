# Perfect Competition — Decision Memo

## 1. Recommendation

I recommend producing 10 tomato beds, 20 carrot beds, and 30 mesclun beds. This combination is the model's optimized plan and produces total profit of approximately $42,761.66 (`Optimization!B6:B11`).

## 2. Why this plan

The plan follows the marginal decision rule of producing additional beds when price exceeds marginal cost. Tomatoes stop at 10 beds because the marginal cost of bed 11 exceeds the $8,800 price. Carrots and mesclun are produced to their bed caps because their constraints bind, while each crop still covers its variable cost and contributes toward the farm's fixed cost. The plan uses 60 total beds out of 64 available and 3.1647 temporary workers out of 4, so those overall constraints remain slack.

## 3. Judgment call: which cap to relax first

I would relax the carrot cap first. Increasing the carrot limit from 20 to 21 beds increased optimized profit by $352.49, while increasing the mesclun limit from 30 to 31 beds increased profit by $246.47. The carrot cap therefore has the greater shadow price and offers the larger immediate return from expansion. The $352.49 is the value of relaxing the carrot cap by one bed, from 20 to 21. It is not a constant that can be applied automatically to every further carrot bed, and any expansion beyond bed 21 would need to be re-evaluated at the new margin.

## 4. What would change the recommendation

The recommendation should be recalculated if crop prices, farmer or temporary-labor rates, bed limits, or available labor change. A substantial increase in tomato price or decrease in tomato labor cost could justify additional tomato beds. Likewise, if mesclun's additional-bed profit became greater than carrots' or another constraint became binding, the priority for expanding production could change.
